# Misdeeds

> [!NOTE]
> This is a planned feature. It is not in the game yet, and details may change before it arrives.

## What it is for

Levels alone don't make a creature worth protecting: one level 60 Orc is the same as any other level 60 Orc.
Misdeeds makes each creature its own: the game remembers what it did, rewards it for that, lets you name it,
and gives you a reason to bring it home when it falls. A creature you raised for weeks should be something you
can't get back by training a replacement.

The names follow the game's joke that evil is the virtue. A creature's proudest achievements read like a charge
sheet.

| Part | Name |
|---|---|
| The whole system, and the record of what a creature did | **Misdeeds** |
| Traits earned from misdeeds | **Vices** |
| The new tab that shows a creature's record | **Rap Sheet** |
| Naming a creature | **Rename** |
| A veteran speeding up newcomers' training | **Bad Influence** |
| A fallen veteran you can carry home | **Unfinished Business** |
| A lost veteran's vice passing to a newcomer | **Bad Blood** |

## 1. Misdeeds: the game remembers

Every creature keeps a small set of counters for its whole life. They are invisible while they count, and
everything else on this page reads from them.

- Kills, split by what was killed: heroes, wild creatures, other keepers' creatures.
- Damage soaked.
- Fights at night.
- Time spent in lava and in water.
- Hours worked: training, research, manufacture, guarding, and digging for imps.
- Farthest distance from the heart, and total distance travelled away from home.
- Outposts and dungeon hearts it helped take.
- Battles survived, and times it fell.
- The day it joined the dungeon.

The counters are saved with the creature and stay with it for good.

## 2. Vices: earned from misdeeds, picked by you

At levels **10, 25, 50, 75 and 100** a creature earns a vice. Its two strongest misdeeds each offer one, and
the keeper picks which to keep. A creature ends with five.

Vices are situational, never plain stat boosts: levels already supply the raw power. The pick matters because
it lets you raise creatures differently on purpose. The Orc you want to be Territorial is the one you keep
home to defend.

| Vice | Earned by | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Do-Gooder's Bane** | Killing heroes | +20% damage to heroes. |
| **Territorial** | Fighting on your own land | Takes hits for weaker creatures nearby while on your land. |
| **Afraid of Nothing in the Dark** | Fighting at night | Faster and harder to see at night. |
| **Asbestos Hide** | Time in lava | Walks through lava unharmed. |
| **Knows a Shortcut** | Distance travelled away from home | The group it travels with moves faster away from home. |

This is a starting set. Every kind of misdeed should end up with at least one vice, so that workers and
home-bodies earn theirs too.

## 3. Rename: a creature that reaches level 10 can be named

- Reaching level 10 unlocks Rename for that creature.
- The creature's stats tab shows a **pencil icon** beside its name. Clicking it lets the keeper type a new
  name.
- A creature can be renamed **once**. After that the pencil is gone and the name is permanent.
- When the keeper **hovers the hand over the creature**, its name appears **above its health flower**.

Level 10 is also where the first vice arrives, so it is the moment a creature stops being one of the crowd.

## 4. Rap Sheet: a new tab for the question mark tool

Selecting a creature with the question mark tool opens its information panel. That panel gets one more tab,
the Rap Sheet, and it is where everything in this system can be seen in one place:

- The creature's name, and the pencil if it can still be renamed.
- The day it joined, and how long it has served.
- Its misdeeds, laid out like a list of prior convictions.
- Its vices, each with what it does, and empty slots showing the level at which the next one arrives.
- When a vice is waiting to be picked, the two choices are offered here.

The existing tabs stay as they are. The Rap Sheet only adds.

## 5. Bad Influence: veterans pay off at home

A creature 20 or more levels above others of its kind in the Training Room speeds up their training. The old
guard shortens the grind for every newcomer, so a veteran is valuable even when it isn't fighting.

## 6. Unfinished Business and Bad Blood: loss hurts but isn't the end

- **Unfinished Business.** A creature with at least two vices leaves a body that lasts one day. Carried home
  to the Graveyard, it returns one vice poorer. A death becomes a rescue trip.
- **Bad Blood.** If it is lost for good, the next creature of that kind the keeper attracts inherits one of
  its vices when it reaches level 10.

## Order of arrival

1. Misdeeds, the Rap Sheet and Rename.
2. Vices.
3. Bad Influence.
4. Unfinished Business, then Bad Blood.

## Still being decided

- Whether vices count in fights between keepers, or only against heroes and wild creatures.
- On shared servers: whether other players see a creature's name, how long a name can be, and whether a
  server's staff can clear one.
