# Creatures

> [!NOTE]
> These are planned features. They are not in the game yet, and details may change before they arrive.

## What it is for

Your creatures fight, work and sleep, and little more is asked of you about them. These ideas give you choices
about each one: what to do with a captive, how a creature is paid, what it gives you when you take it over, and
which of its habits you put up with.

## A prisoner's fate

Each captive gets a real choice instead of only "convert or starve":

- **Ransom**: gold now, and the hero comes back in a later raid a level higher.
- **Interrogate**: it tells you what it knows, such as its stronghold's hidden traps and portal rooms, or the
  nearest place you have not found yet.
- **Convert**, **starve into a Skeleton**, or **sacrifice**, as today.

Interrogation is always worth less than converting a Knight.

## Contracts

Every ten levels a creature renegotiates its pay, and you choose how:

- **Gold**, as now.
- **A share of plunder**, and no wage.
- **Bed and board**: a bigger lair and double food, and no wage.
- **Glory**: no wage while it gets a kill each day, and fury when it doesn't.

Armies can be built as mercenaries, zealots or gluttons. Each kind has a default, so you are asked only about the
creatures you have named.

## Seeing through their eyes

Taking over a creature gets a reason for each kind. For example:

- A possessed Fly sees hidden traps.
- An Imp digs three times as fast where you point, and can carry a book home.
- A Warlock's aimed shots strike harder.
- A Dark Mistress lends her Lightning to the party.
- A Vampire reads a hero's level and purse.

Kills made this way show on the creature's [Rap Sheet](MISDEEDS.md) as "Driven".

## Virtues

The mirror of [vices](MISDEEDS.md), and in this game a virtue is a flaw. For example:

- A creature whose record shows running away earns **Prudence**, and runs sooner.
- One that slept through raids earns **Temperance**, and trains more slowly.
- One that only ever knocks enemies out earns **Mercy**, and will not finish them.

A spell on the rack corrects one virtue, at the cost of the creature's work for a day.
